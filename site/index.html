nightbell

The after-hours line for water damage restoration. It answers on the first ring, runs a real intake, and wakes your tech only when it should.

In private beta with restoration contractors

A burst supply line at 2am is a five to fifteen thousand dollar job. It goes to whoever picks up the phone. Most nights, that is not you — it is the franchise with a call centre, or the competitor two towns over whose owner sleeps with the phone on the pillow.

An answering service does not fix this. They take a name and a number and a vague note about water, and you still have to call the customer back and have the actual conversation before you can decide whether to get dressed. The intake is the work. Handing it to someone who does not know what category three water is just moves the work to 6am.

What it actually does

nightbell picks up, works through the full intake a restoration job needs, and puts a complete ticket in front of you. By the time you read it, you know whether to roll a truck.

The whole intake

Callback number first, then source, category, affected areas, standing water, access, carrier and claim number. Not a name and a message.

Triage you can audit

Sewage, an active leak, a sagging ceiling, a newborn in the house — each escalates by a fixed rule, and every escalation shows you which rule fired and why.

It knows when to stop

Gas, sparking, someone hurt, or a caller who just wants a person — it transfers immediately instead of trying to be helpful.

You can interrupt it

Talk over it and it stops, the way a person does. Callers standing in water do not wait politely for a prompt to finish.

A call, as it happens

2:14am — Ring.

nightbell: Buckeye Restoration, this is the after-hours line. What's the best number to reach you on, in case we get cut off?

Caller: It's five one two, five five five, oh one four four. There's water everywhere, I don't know what happened.

nightbell: Got it. Tell me what's happening.

Caller: I came downstairs and the whole basement's got — I think it's the sewer, it smells awful, and it's still coming up.

nightbell: Okay. I'm treating this as an emergency and getting someone out to you tonight. What's the address?

↑ Escalated to dispatch — category 3 (sewage) + water still active. On-call paged at 2:14am.

The escalation is not a judgement call the software made in the moment. Those two conditions are written down, they fire the same way on every call, and the reasoning is attached to the record.

What you get in the morning

  • A job ticket with every intake field filled in
  • The full transcript and the recording
  • Which triage rules fired, and the caller's exact words that fired them
  • An SMS to the caller confirming someone is coming

The transcript matters more than it sounds. You are letting something answer your phone at 2am; you should be able to read back exactly what it told a customer, and so should your adjuster.
